# Campus Customs: Design (Problem 10)

**Concept:** an engraved autumn catalogue from a small New Haven shop, in the spirit of the J. Peterman catalogue. It should feel like something you'd want to linger over, not a discount rack. Everything below supports one goal: **keep shoppers browsing, help them trust what they see, and make buying feel easy.**

## Finishing touches added in Problem 10

| Change | What it is | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Engraved Yale skyline masthead** | A grand, steel-engraving-style panorama of Harkness Tower, Sterling Memorial Library, Collegiate Gothic rooftops and elms in Yale-blue ink. It runs full width above the home page's blue band (the tree line rests on the band, and the ends fade softly into the paper) and appears pale across the footer like a letterhead. On phones it crops in on Harkness and Sterling. The artwork was generated in ChatGPT from our written prompt, then we removed the white paper so the ink sits on the page, made a pale footer version, and compressed both for fast loading. An earlier hand-coded line drawing looked too much like modern clip art, so it was replaced. | Gives the store a signature image that says "Yale" without a logo. A rich, crafted engraving signals quality and heritage, which helps justify premium prices and makes the store memorable. |
| **Tidier product names** | Quarter-zips read "1/4 Zip" instead of the database's "1 4 Zip," everywhere: catalogue, bag, chat and the fact-checker. | Small typos make a store look careless. Clean names keep the polish consistent. |
| **Gentle entrance motion** | On the home page the headline, text and buttons settle in, and the catalogue "plates" drop into place. Product cards fade up as you scroll, with neighbors slightly staggered like turning a page. | Draws the eye down the page and rewards scrolling. More scrolling means more products seen. |
| **Paw "stamp"** | Saving to the wishlist presses the paw like an ink stamp, with a faint ring. | Instant, satisfying confirmation ("it worked"), which encourages saving and coming back. |
| **Handsome Dan comes alive** | He blinks every few seconds (his two portraits out of sync), tilts his head while thinking (only the head moves, like a dog listening), and the typing dots read *"Sniffing through the stockroom…"* | A lifelike, understated mascot makes the assistant feel warm and attentive, and makes waiting for an answer feel shorter. |
| **Quick look** | Hovering a catalogue card shows "Available in S, M, L and XL." On phones (no hover) the same line sits quietly under the price. | Answers the #1 question (is it in my size?) without a click, so shoppers reach the right item faster. |
| **Calmer sold-out display** | Removed the big red "SOLD OUT" banner and ribbons. Only the small notice on each sold-out size remains. | Keeps product pages elegant and focused on what *is* available, rather than shouting about what isn't. |
| **Polish and phone pass** | Tightened spacing around the masthead; checked every page on a phone with no sideways scrolling. | Most shoppers browse on phones, and a polished mobile experience keeps them there. |
| **Respectful motion** | All animation switches off automatically for people whose device asks for reduced motion. | Accessible for everyone, including people who get motion-sick. |

## Design choices made along the way

| Choice | Details | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Yale blue and white palette** | Yale blue `#00356b` with a brighter Yale blue accent, warm white "paper" background, ink-navy text, one red reserved for sold-out notices. (A deliberate exception to the course workspace's black-and-pink default, because the assignment asked us to match the Campus Customs style.) | Matches the real store and the university; restrained color reads as premium and keeps attention on the clothes. |
| **Editorial typography** | Cormorant Garamond for headlines (catalogue elegance), EB Garamond for body text, Libre Franklin in small caps for labels, prices and buttons. Fonts are bundled with the site, not loaded from Google. | Feels like a printed catalogue, with clear hierarchy (name → story → price) and no third-party tracking. |
| **J. Peterman catalogue voice** | Every product has an original catalogue-style tagline and story, alongside the plain facts ("The Particulars"). Pages read "No. 012," "Plate I," "The Autumn Ledger." | Storytelling makes ordinary sweatshirts feel special and worth the price, while the facts stay honest. |
| **Catalogue presentation** | Numbered entries, framed product "plates," tilted plates on the home page, a pull-quote band, four collections (Colleges, Varsity, Family, Professional Schools). | Browsing feels like leafing through a catalogue rather than scrolling a spreadsheet. |
| **Handsome Dan as the chat** | A hand-drawn bulldog in a Yale sweater with a speech bubble ("What can I help you with?") in the bottom right of every page. Replies come with product cards and a "Checked against the stockroom" badge. | A friendly, on-brand guide lowers the effort of finding things, and the badge builds trust in what he says. |
| **Paw prints and a bone** | The paw is the wishlist button and menu icon; "Handsome Dan's Picks" uses a bone icon and faint paw prints on a dashed panel. | Playful mascot details tie features to Dan without cheapening the look. |
| **Clear sold-out honesty** | Sold-out sizes are crossed out with a small red notice and can't be selected. | Shoppers never discover a problem at checkout, and trust grows. |
| **Low-friction shopping** | Filters with color swatches and "in my size," the wishlist, the bag, Recently viewed, Dan's Picks on the page, an in-chat account button. | Every shortcut removes a reason to leave. |

## What we deliberately avoided

Pop-ups, countdown timers, flashing sales banners, autoplaying video and cartoonish animation. They feel cheap and pushy, and they don't fit a store "worthy of the Yale name."
